# Background: Know Your Customer (KYC)

Know Your Customer (KYC) is the collection of laws, regulations and guidelines
that require regulated financial businesses to verify the identity, suitability
and risks associated with maintaining a business relationship with a customer.
These procedures fit within the broader scope of Anti-Money Laundering (AML)
and Counter-Terrorism Financing (CTF) regulations.

Modern KYC frameworks are built on three pillars:

- **Customer Identification Program (CIP)** — legally mandated verification of
  any person or entity opening a new account, including name, date of birth,
  address and a government-issued identification number.
- **Customer Due Diligence (CDD)** — understanding the nature and purpose of a
  client relationship, developing a risk profile, and ongoing monitoring of
  transactions for suspicious activity.
- **Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)** — extra checks for higher risk clients such
  as politically exposed persons (PEPs), cross-border correspondent banking
  relationships, or businesses operating in jurisdictions with strategic
  deficiencies in their AML/CFT regimes.

Regulated entities are required to maintain a written, risk-based KYC program
that: forms a reasonable belief that they know the true identity of every
customer; verifies identity within a reasonable time; checks the customer
against sanctions and terror watchlists; and retains records for a defined
period after account closure (typically five years).

Electronic KYC (eKYC) uses digital identity verification, biometric checks and
liveness detection to onboard customers remotely. In India, the Reserve Bank of
India first issued KYC guidelines for banks in 2002; video KYC (V-CIP) is
permitted for eligible savings account onboarding, subject to procedural
controls around live capture, random challenge and geo-tagging.
