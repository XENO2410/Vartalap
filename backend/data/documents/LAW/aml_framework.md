# Anti-Money Laundering (AML) — Operating Framework

## Purpose
The AML framework prevents the bank's channels being used to launder proceeds
of crime and to finance terrorism, in line with the Prevention of Money
Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002 and RBI Master Direction on KYC.

## Three Lines of Defence
- **1st line — Business units:** onboard customers correctly, monitor
  transactions, escalate red flags.
- **2nd line — Financial Crime Compliance (FCC):** owns the AML policy,
  runs the sanctions and PEP screening tools, files STRs.
- **3rd line — Internal Audit:** independently tests the framework annually.

## Customer Risk Categorisation
- **Low risk:** salaried resident individuals with no adverse hits.
- **Medium risk:** self-employed, high-net-worth individuals, non-resident
  individuals with low transaction velocity.
- **High risk:** NGOs, cash-intensive businesses, PEPs, customers in
  FATF-listed jurisdictions.

Risk category drives periodic KYC refresh frequency (see the KYC standard).

## Reporting Obligations
| Report | Threshold | Filing SLA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash Transaction Report (CTR) | Cash > ₹10L / day per customer | 15th of the following month |
| Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) | On red flag | 7 working days from detection |
| Counterfeit Currency Report (CCR) | Any incident | 7 working days |
| Cross-Border Wire Transfer Report (CBWTR) | > ₹5L | 15th of the following month |

## Training
All customer-facing staff must complete the annual AML e-learning by
30 September of each year. Non-completion is tracked as an audit finding.
